How these numbers are calculated
  • BMI = weight ÷ height². Classes follow WHO (18.5 / 25 / 30 / 35).
  • Body fat % = 1.20·BMI + 0.23·age − 10.8·(male) − 5.4 (Deurenberg et al., 1991). Typical error against DXA is ±4–5 percentage points; it can't see whether you carry more muscle than average.
  • Lean mass = weight − fat mass. Muscle is a rough share of lean mass (52% male, 47% female), shown for trends only.
  • Body water assumes lean tissue is 73% water (Wang et al., 1999).
  • FFMI / FMI = lean or fat mass ÷ height² (Kouri 1995; VanItallie 1990); normalised FFMI adjusts to 1.8 m.
  • BMR: Mifflin–St Jeor (1990). Ideal weight: Devine, Robinson, Miller, Hamwi; optimal here = BMI 22.
  • Fat categories: ACE ranges; healthy bands: Gallagher et al. (2000), by age and sex.
  • The scale's raw electrical impedance is stored but not used, because its vendor algorithm is unpublished.
Informational only, not medical advice. Day-to-day weight swings of 1–2 kg from water and food are normal, so judge progress by the trend rather than single readings.

Heart rate variability

What is HRV?

Your heartbeat is not a metronome. The time between beats varies by a few milliseconds, and heart rate variability (HRV) measures how much. Apple Health records it as SDNN, the spread of those gaps, in milliseconds.

Higher usually means your nervous system is relaxed and recovered; lower means it is under load from hard training, stress, illness, alcohol or short sleep.

How to read the chart

  • Dots are daily averages; the thick line smooths them over 7 readings.
  • The shaded band is your own normal range (average ± 0.75 standard deviations). Inside it is ordinary day-to-day noise.
  • The dashed line is your 7-day baseline, the same one the Overview tile compares against.

What matters

  • Compare with yourself, not other people: HRV differs a lot by age, genetics and device, so 40 ms can be normal for one person and 90 ms for another.
  • Trends beat single days. One low reading is common; several days below the band suggest you need rest.
  • Below 80% of baseline we say go easy; at or above 95% you are at baseline.
  • Helpers: consistent sleep, easy aerobic exercise, less alcohol, managing stress.

Not medical advice. Talk to a doctor about persistent changes or symptoms.
